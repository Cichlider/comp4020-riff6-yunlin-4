## The brief

Build one piece of persuasive work --- an essay, a design, a short film,
a data visualisation, a speech, anything that argues a position --- whose
persuasive force depends on something it deliberately never states
outright. Submit the piece alongside a 600 to 1000 word critical
commentary: what the omission is, why the argument needs it unstated
rather than stated, and how it holds up against week 12's counter-case
that not every omission is doing real work.

The commentary must engage at least three weeks of the semester's
material by name, and must directly address the possibility that your
own omission is just a gap, not a decision --- the same test week 12
puts to the whole course.

## Submission format

The piece itself, in whatever format the medium requires, plus the
commentary as a separate document. If the piece depends on being read,
watched or heard in a specific order, say so in one line at the top of
the commentary.

## What this is testing

The semester's central claim, applied to your own work under scrutiny:
can you make a cut that does real argumentative work, and can you tell
the difference between that and a cut that's just missing something,
in your own piece, when you're the one grading yourself first.
