Classical continuity editing --- the 180-degree rule, shot/reverse shot,
match on action --- is a set of techniques for hiding the cut inside the
story rather than showing it as itself. It's the most successful
disappearing act in this course: most audiences watching a
conventionally-edited film couldn't tell you where a single cut fell,
because the whole system is built to make that question feel irrelevant.

Compare the Soviet montage tradition, which does the opposite on purpose:
Eisenstein wanted the collision between two shots to be the meaning, not
something to smooth past. The lecture uses the Kuleshov effect --- the
same shot of an actor's neutral face, read as hunger, grief or desire
depending purely on what it's cut next to --- as the clearest demonstration
in the whole semester that a cut can generate meaning neither shot had
alone, which is a stronger claim than anything painting or theology has
offered so far.
