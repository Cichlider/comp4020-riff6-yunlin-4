A redacted government document is the most literal object in this
course: black bars over specific words, the omission itself printed and
handed to you. But the more interesting cases are the ones where nothing
is visibly missing. An economic model that leaves a cost --- carbon,
care work, noise --- out of its accounting isn't hiding anything; it's
just declining to count it, and the model still balances. A political
speech that never states its actual policy, only its opponent's,
persuades by omission while looking like a complete argument.

This week is the direct rehearsal for the final assessment: an argument
whose force depends on what it refuses to say is a redaction with the
black bars removed, structurally the same move as everything else in
weeks 2 through 10, just with higher stakes for who gets to decide what
counts as background.

## Outline

- redaction as the literal case: the omission you can see the shape of
- unpriced externalities: omission that doesn't look like omission at all
