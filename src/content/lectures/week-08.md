Dieter Rams's tenth principle --- "good design is as little design as
possible" --- gets quoted constantly and read carelessly almost as often,
usually as a licence for blankness. Rams's own products argue otherwise:
the Braun T3 radio and the SK4 record player took things out (visible
fixings, ornament, unnecessary controls) from designs that used to have
them, and the resulting object is legible specifically because you can
feel the decision, not because nothing is there.

The lecture's central distinction: a design that subtracted something
still carries the shape of what it removed, the way a scar carries the
shape of a wound; a design that simply never had the feature was never
making an argument at all. Most bad "minimalist" design is the second
kind wearing the first kind's reputation.

## Outline

- Rams's tenth principle, and the two ways to misread it
- Braun's T3/SK4 as objects that show their own subtraction
- telling "removed" from "never there" in your own studio work so far
